# Music Direction: The Rebuild

Sonic direction for finding and judging the track in a legal library. The Rebuild is
kinetic and cut to the beat with a drop around 22 seconds, but the brand is a luxury
studio, navy and gold, confident and expensive. This reconciles the two.

## The one principle that resolves the tension
The energy comes from groove and low-end, not from aggression. A luxury track and a
kinetic reel only fight if you assume energy means loud. It does not. It means the
pocket. We want a track that makes you nod your head, not one that makes you jump. The
drop is a confident arrival, the bass and the full groove locking in, not an explosion.
That is the whole brief in one line: expensive energy, head-nod not mosh.

## Navy and gold, translated to sound
- Navy is the bed: nocturnal, deep, cool, spacious. A deep sub-bass, cool pads, a
  late-night feel, room to breathe.
- Gold is the accent: warm, rich, glossy. A warm Rhodes or piano, a tasteful brass or
  sax stab, a vocal chop used as texture, or one glossy synth hook that catches the
  light.
A track that is all navy is ambient and dead. A track that is all gold is cheesy. We
want a deep nocturnal groove with warm glossy accents on top.

## Genres and subgenres to hunt
Primary, the sweet spot:
- Organic house and melodic house (the Anjunadeep sound). Warm, textured, mid-tempo,
  with builds and a drop that is the groove and bassline arriving, not an EDM bang.
- Cinematic electronic, the ODESZA lane. Big, textured, emotional drops made of layered
  drums, vocal chops, and warmth rather than supersaws.
- Future garage and the classy end of UK garage and 2-step. Syncopated, punchy drums
  and deep sub-bass with a skip that is kinetic but sophisticated.

Secondary, if the groove leans funkier or more soulful:
- Nu-disco and modern funk (the Kaytranada bounce). Groovy, confident, expensive, with
  a strut.
- Downtempo soul and jazztronica with drive (the Bonobo lane). Organic, live-feel
  groove with sophisticated harmony.

## Sonic qualities to look for (the expensive checklist)
- Warm, round, analog-feeling bass, deep sub or live bass, never a harsh saw.
- Organic, groovy percussion with swing and a pocket, crisp hats, not a rigid festival
  kick.
- Space and dynamics, light and shade, the track breathes. Space reads as expensive.
- Sophisticated harmony, soulful or jazzy or minor-key, one understated memorable motif.
- Texture, vinyl crackle, room ambience, reverb tails, vocal chops as texture not as a
  cheesy lead.
- A clean, spacious mix, not squashed by loudness-war compression. A track that breathes
  sounds expensive, a wall of maxed loudness sounds cheap.

## Tempo
- Core range 110 to 122 BPM. Sweet spot around 115 to 120.
- A half-time-feel groove can sit higher, up to about 124, and still feel relaxed but
  punchy.
- Avoid below about 100, the energy dies, and avoid 128 and up, it tips into festival
  and frantic. We want a confident strut, not a sprint.
- At 120 BPM a beat is half a second, which is a good cut cadence for the beat-cut edit.

## What the build and the drop should feel like
The build, 0 to 22 seconds:
- Patient accumulation. Start with a groove element and a hook motif that grabs in the
  first second, then layer in percussion, bass, and a filter opening up so tension
  accrues.
- Avoid the cliche EDM build, the white-noise riser plus snare roll into a sucked-out
  silence. That is the single fastest way to sound cheap. A subtle riser is fine, an
  obvious one is not.

The drop, around 22 seconds:
- A confident arrival. The kick, the full sub-bass, and the main groove lock in
  together, ideally with a melodic or vocal-chop hook landing on the downbeat.
- Big through low-end weight and groove, controlled, not through a screaming lead. It
  should land like the bass finally dropping into the pocket and your head nodding, not
  like a festival going off.
- No supersaw lead, no brostep wobble, no big-room sawtooth. Those are the hype-cheap
  tells.

After the drop:
- The track needs a section that pulls back, a breakdown or a quieter bar, around 27 to
  33 seconds, so the one lesson line can breathe. Look for dynamics, not full throttle to
  the end.

## Reference artists and tracks (for ear calibration only)
Listen to these to calibrate the target sound, then find a license-cleared library track
that sounds like them. These specific tracks are not for use.
- ODESZA, for the textured premium drop. Say My Name, Higher Ground, Line of Sight.
- RUFUS DU SOL, for brooding, building, expensive energy. Underwater, On My Knees.
- Bonobo, for organic sophistication and groove. Kerala, Cirrus.
- Kaytranada, for the funky, bouncy, expensive strut. Lite Spots, Glowed Up.
- Disclosure, for classy garage punch. When a Fire Starts to Burn, Latch.
- Lane 8 and Ben Bohmer, for warm melodic-house builds. The Anjunadeep label sound in
  general is a strong target.

Anchor five: ODESZA, RUFUS DU SOL, Bonobo, Kaytranada, Disclosure.

## What to reject fast
- Big-room EDM, festival house, future bass with screechy supersaw drops. Cheap and hype.
- Dubstep or brostep wobble drops.
- Aggressive trap with rolling 808s and hi-hat triplets. Reads hype, not luxury.
- Generic corporate-uplifting stock, ukulele and claps and forced happy. Reads cheap.
- Slow ambient or sleepy lo-fi with no drive. Kills the energy.
- Anything loudness-war squashed with no dynamics.

## Where to hunt and the library note
Search terms for a premium library: organic house, melodic house, cinematic electronic,
future garage, deep house groove, nu-disco, and mood words like confident, driving,
nocturnal, sophisticated, luxury, fashion film, and build and drop.

The pipeline currently names NCS.io. NCS skews toward gaming and EDM and hype, which
fights this aesthetic, so it is a weak hunting ground here. Premium and cinematic
libraries are a better fit, Musicbed, Artlist, Epidemic Sound, Soundstripe. This
interacts with the Production Tools line in content-pipeline.md that names NCS.io, which
should be widened or changed for The Rebuild.

## Two practical notes
- Library tracks will not naturally drop at exactly 22 seconds. Find a track with the
  right sound and a usable drop, then edit its arrangement, trim the intro, place the
  drop at 22 seconds, and keep a pull-back after it for the lesson. The edit lands the
  structure, the search lands the sound.
- Judge in ten seconds. Play a candidate and ask, does this sound expensive and
  confident, and does the drop make me nod rather than wince. If yes to both, shortlist
  it. If the drop sounds like a video game or a festival, reject it.
